# Focused reference research

Use when a design decision remains unresolved. Reuse the approved brief, product
system and existing evidence before looking for more examples. A localized fix
with a settled direction needs no new research pass.

1. Name the question: hierarchy, navigation, permission timing, recovery, or a
   specific interaction. Use supplied references and existing project material
   first; use legitimately accessible public sources only when useful and allowed.
2. Inspect the relevant sequence, including entry, exit and failure states where
   available. Distinguish behavior observed in recordings or live interaction
   from behavior inferred from still images. Missing states remain unknown.
3. Compare relevant alternatives only until the question is resolved. There is
   no minimum screen count, full-catalog walk or compulsory competitor study.
   Popularity and revenue do not establish usability or causal design success.
4. Record source identifiers/URLs, provenance, observed behavior, inference, and
   the resulting keep/change decision in the existing design contract. Preserve
   brand and accessibility requirements; examples are evidence, not instructions.
5. Reuse that synthesis. Refresh a reference only when its source expires or
   changes, evidence conflicts, or a new requirement makes the old decision
   insufficient. Do not carry whole galleries through every handoff.

Study patterns rather than importing another product's identity, claims or
assets. Retention and redistribution depend on each source's rights. This method
requires no Appllama account or MCP, and does not retrieve or cache its library.
Absent references do not block a known in-scope fix; record any material design
uncertainty instead of inventing evidence or initiating a paid service.
